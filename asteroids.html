Asteroids

What are Asteroids?

Asteroids are rocky, airless remnants left over from the early formation of our solar system about 4.6 billion years ago. Often called minor planets, they are smaller than traditional planets but orbit the Sun just like them.

The Asteroid Belt

The vast majority of known asteroids orbit the Sun within the cosmic region known as the Asteroid Belt, which is located between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. This belt contains millions of rocky bodies of all shapes and sizes.

Types of Asteroids

C-type (Carbonaceous) These are the most common, making up about 75% of known asteroids. They are extremely dark and consist mostly of ancient carbon-rich rocks.
S-type (Stony) Making up about 17% of asteroids, these are composed mainly of silicate materials and nickel-iron, making them relatively bright.
M-type (Metallic) These are rare and consist almost entirely of pure nickel-iron. They are thought to be the shattered cores of ancient proto-planets.

Quick Comparison Table
