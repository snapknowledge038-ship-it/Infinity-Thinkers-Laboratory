Dark Matter & Dark Energy

The Invisible Universe

Everything we can see, touch, and observe with telescopes—stars, planets, gas clouds, and entire galaxies—makes up only about 5% of the universe. The remaining 95% is completely invisible, made up of two mysterious components: Dark Matter and Dark Energy.

What is Dark Matter?

Dark Matter makes up roughly 27% of the universe. It does not emit, absorb, or reflect light, making it completely invisible to our instruments. We only know it exists because of its massive gravitational pull. Without Dark Matter acting as an invisible "cosmic glue," galaxies would fly apart as they rotate.

What is Dark Energy?

Dominating the cosmos at around 68%, Dark Energy is an enigmatic force embedded in the very fabric of space. While gravity pulls matter together, Dark Energy acts as a repulsive force that drives the universe apart, causing the expansion of the cosmos to accelerate faster and faster over time.

The Cosmic Breakdown

Normal Matter (~5%) The atomic matter that forms people, Earth, the Sun, and all regular cosmic structures.
Dark Matter (~27%) The unseen scaffolding of the universe. It pulls regular matter together to build galaxies.
Dark Energy (~68%) The mysterious pressure pushing space apart, determining the ultimate fate of our universe.
