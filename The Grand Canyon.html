The Grand Canyon

The Grand Canyon is a steep-sided canyon carved by the Colorado River in Arizona, United States. It is one of the most studied geological landscapes in the world.

A Journey Through Time

The canyon is nearly 1,500 meters deep and stretches for 446 kilometers. The layers of rock exposed in the canyon walls reveal nearly two billion years of Earth's geological history.

Breathtaking Facts

  • Dynamic Weather: The temperature at the top (Rim) can be freezing, while the bottom of the canyon feels like a hot desert.
  • Unique Ecosystem: It is home to rare animals like the California Condor and the Bighorn Sheep.
  • Native History: For thousands of years, the area has been continuously inhabited by Native Americans, who built settlements within the canyon.

The Power of Water

While it looks like a silent monument, the Grand Canyon is still changing. The Colorado River continues to erode the rock, deepening the canyon by the thickness of a sheet of paper every year.
