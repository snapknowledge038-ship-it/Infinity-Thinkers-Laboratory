The Great Barrier Reef

The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system, composed of over 2,900 individual reefs and 900 islands. It is so vast that it can be seen from Outer Space.

Location & Wonders

Located in the Coral Sea, off the coast of Queensland, Australia, this natural wonder stretches over 2,300 kilometers. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the seven natural wonders of the world.

A Vibrant Underwater City

The reef is home to a breathtaking array of marine life:

  • Marine Biodiversity: It hosts 1,500 species of fish, 400 types of hard coral, and one-third of the world's soft corals.
  • Majestic Creatures: Six of the world's seven species of marine turtles come to the reef to breed.
  • Giant Inhabitants: It provides a vital breeding ground for humpback whales migrating from the Antarctic.

The Threat of Climate Change

The reef faces its greatest threat from rising ocean temperatures, which cause Coral Bleaching. When the water is too warm, corals expel the algae living in their tissues, turning them white and putting them at risk of dying.
