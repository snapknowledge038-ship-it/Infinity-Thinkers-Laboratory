Neutron Stars

What is a Neutron Star?

A neutron star is the collapsed core of a massive supergiant star that has run out of fuel and exploded in a violent supernova. Though they are only about the size of a city (around 20 kilometers in diameter), they hold more mass than our Sun, making them some of the densest objects in the universe.

What is a Pulsar?

A pulsar is a highly magnetized, rapidly spinning neutron star. As it rotates, it shoots intense beams of electromagnetic radiation out of its magnetic poles. To an observer on Earth, these beams look like a cosmic lighthouse, appearing as regular, rhythmic pulses of light.

Extreme Characteristics

Mind-Boggling Density The matter in a neutron star is packed so tightly that a single teaspoon of it would weigh about 1 billion tons on Earth—roughly equal to the weight of Mount Everest!
Incredible Rotation Some pulsars can rotate hundreds of times per second. The fastest known millisecond pulsar spins at a staggering 716 times every single second.
Immense Gravity The surface gravity of a neutron star is extremely powerful. If an object were to fall onto a neutron star, it would hit the surface at millions of miles per hour.

Types of Neutron Stars

Type Key Feature
Radio Pulsars Neutron stars that emit steady, periodic radio waves detected by Earth's radio telescopes.
Magnetars A rare type of neutron star with an ultra-powerful magnetic field capable of wiping information from a credit card from thousands of miles away.
X-ray Pulsars Pulsars that pull material from a companion star, heating it up to emit intense X-ray blasts.
