Mariana Trench

The Mariana Trench is the deepest part of the world's oceans. Located in the western Pacific Ocean, it is a crescent-shaped scar in the Earth’s crust that measures about 2,550 km long.

Challenger Deep

The deepest known point is the Challenger Deep. It reaches a depth of approximately 10,935 meters (35,876 feet) below sea level. If Mount Everest were dropped into the trench, its peak would still be over 2 kilometers underwater.

Extreme Conditions

  • Crushing Pressure: At the bottom, the pressure is over 1,000 times the standard atmospheric pressure at sea level. It's like having an elephant stand on your thumb.
  • Freezing Temperatures: The water temperature at the bottom fluctuates between 1°C and 4°C.
  • Eternal Darkness: No sunlight reaches these depths, creating a world of perpetual night.

Life in the Void

Despite the extreme pressure and cold, life exists here. Scientists have discovered xenophyophores, amphipods, and small sea cucumbers that have adapted to survive in one of the harshest environments on the planet.
