The Hope Diamond

Weighing an astonishing 45.52 carats, The Hope Diamond is one of the most famous and visually stunning gemstones in human history. However, beneath its mesmerizing deep-blue glow lies a dark and terrifying reputation as a cursed jewel that brings tragedy, madness, and death to anyone who owns it.

The Theft from the Idol

The legend begins in India, where the diamond was allegedly stolen from the eye of a sacred statue of the goddess Sita. According to folklore, the priests of the temple placed a severe malediction on the thief and all future keepers of the stone, preconditoning a lifetime of absolute ruin.

A Trail of Royal Blood

The diamond found its way to France, where it was bought by King Louis XIV. Shortly after, the French Royal Family faced extreme misfortune. Both King Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette owned the jewel during the French Revolution, and both famously met their grim fates at the guillotine.

The Hope Family Misfortune

  • The Namesake Curse: In 1839, the jewel was bought by Henry Philip Hope, giving the stone its modern name. Soon after, the family’s immense wealth completely evaporated into bankruptcy.
  • The McLean Tragedy: Evelyn Walsh McLean bought the diamond in 1911. Tragedy struck quickly; her young son died in a car crash, her husband went insane and died in a mental asylum, and her daughter suffered a fatal drug overdose.
  • The Courier's Fate: Even James Todd, the mail carrier who delivered the diamond to its final destination, had his leg crushed in a truck accident, and his home caught fire shortly after.

The Glowing Phenomenon

  • The Blood-Red Glow: Under short-wave ultraviolet light, the Hope Diamond exhibits a rare scientific feature; it glows a terrifying, fiery phosphorescent red for several seconds after the light source is turned off.
  • The Resting Place: In 1958, jeweler Harry Winston donated the diamond to the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, effectively ending its private ownership and seemingly putting the curse to rest.
