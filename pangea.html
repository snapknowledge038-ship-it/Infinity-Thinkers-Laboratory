Pangea: The Supercontinent

Around 300 million years ago, all the continents we know today were joined together as a single, massive supercontinent. This giant landmass was known as Pangea.

The Giant Ocean & Meaning

In Greek, the word "Pangea" translates to "All Earth". This immense supercontinent was completely surrounded by a single, vast global ocean known as Panthalassa.

How it Broke Apart?

Due to the continuous movement of tectonic plates deep within the Earth, this giant landmass began to break apart into smaller fragments about 175 million years ago.

  • Continental Drift: German scientist Alfred Wegener proposed the Continental Drift Theory to the world in 1912.
  • The Evidence: The most striking evidence was how perfectly the coastlines of different continents fit together like a jigsaw puzzle (for example, South America and Africa).
  • Fossil Matching: Identical species of dinosaur fossils and plant remains were discovered on continents separated by thousands of miles of open ocean.

The Future Map

The movement of these plates has never stopped. They continue to drift toward or away from each other at a rate of a few centimeters per year. Scientists believe that in another 250 million years, all the continents will collide once again to form a new supercontinent called "Pangea Ultima".
