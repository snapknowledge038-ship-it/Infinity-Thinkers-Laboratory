Strings & Wormholes

What is a Wormhole?

A wormhole (officially known as an Einstein-Rosen Bridge) is a theoretical "tunnel" through space-time that could create shortcuts for long journeys across the universe. Predicted by Albert Einstein's theory of general relativity, a wormhole would connect two distant points in space, allowing objects to travel between them faster than light through normal space.

What are Cosmic Strings?

Cosmic strings are hypothetical, ultra-thin defects in the fabric of space-time that may have formed during the early moments of the Big Bang. While thinner than an atom, a single kilometer of a cosmic string could weigh as much as the Earth, packing unimaginable density and gravitational power.

Key Scientific Challenges

Extreme Instability According to physics, standard wormholes would collapse shut instantly the moment any matter tries to pass through them, rendering them impassable.
Exotic Matter Required To keep a wormhole open, scientists theorize we would need "exotic matter" with negative mass and negative energy density to push the tunnel walls outward.
Gravitational Lensing Because cosmic strings are so massive, they would bend light passing near them. Astronomers search for them by looking for duplicate images of distant galaxies.
