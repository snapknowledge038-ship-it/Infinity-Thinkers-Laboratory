Observable Universe

What is the Observable Universe?

The Observable Universe is a spherical region of the universe comprising all matter that can be observed from Earth or its space-based telescopes at the present time. This is because electromagnetic radiation from these objects has had time to reach the Solar System since the beginning of the cosmological expansion.

Mind-Boggling Scale

  • Diameter: It is approximately 93 billion light-years in diameter.
  • The Center: From our perspective, Earth is at the center of the observable universe, but this does not mean Earth is the center of the entire universe.
  • Contents: It is estimated to contain about 2 trillion galaxies and more stars than there are grains of sand on all of Earth's beaches.

The Cosmic Boundary

The further we look into space, the further back in time we see. The edge of what we can see is defined by:
  1. The Cosmological Horizon: The limit beyond which light hasn't had time to reach us yet.
  2. The CMB: The Cosmic Microwave Background radiation, which is the "afterglow" of the Big Bang, marking the limit of visible light.

Universe vs. Observable Universe

Feature Observable Universe Entire Universe
Size 93 Billion Light Years Infinite (or much larger)
Edge Has a visible limit (Horizon) Likely has no edge
Age 13.8 Billion Years 13.8 Billion Years
Shape Spherical (from our view) Flat (according to data)

The Expansion Factor

Even though the universe is only 13.8 billion years old, the observable universe is 93 billion light-years wide. This is because **space itself is expanding**, pushing distant galaxies away from us faster than the light can travel through it.
