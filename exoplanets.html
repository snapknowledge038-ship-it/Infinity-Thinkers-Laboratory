Exoplanets

What is an Exoplanet?

An exoplanet (or extrasolar planet) is a planet that orbits a star outside our own solar system. Until the 1990s, we didn't know for sure if other stars had planets, but today astronomers have discovered thousands of these alien worlds scattered across the Milky Way.

The Habitable Zone

When hunting for exoplanets, scientists look for worlds in the "Habitable Zone" or "Goldilocks Zone." This is the region around a star where temperatures are just right—not too hot and not too cold—for liquid water to exist on a planet's surface, making it a prime candidate for hosting alien life.

Types of Exoplanets

Gas Giants Massive worlds composed mostly of hydrogen and helium, like Jupiter or Saturn. Some are "Hot Jupiters"—giant planets that orbit incredibly close to their stars.
Super-Earths A class of planets unlike anything in our solar system. They are more massive than Earth but lighter than gas giants like Neptune, and can be rocky or gaseous.
Neptune-like Planets similar in size to Neptune and Uranus, featuring atmospheres dominated by hydrogen and helium with icy, rocky cores.

Famous Alien Worlds

Exoplanet Key Characteristic
Kepler-22b The first planet discovered by the Kepler telescope orbiting within the habitable zone of a Sun-like star.
TRAPPIST-1e Part of an amazing system with 7 Earth-sized planets. TRAPPIST-1e is considered one of the most potentially habitable worlds found.
Proxima Centauri b The closest known exoplanet to Earth, located just 4.2 light-years away, orbiting the star nearest to our Sun.
