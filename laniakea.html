Laniakea

What is Laniakea?

Laniakea is the galaxy supercluster that is home to the Milky Way and approximately 100,000 other nearby galaxies. The name means "immense heaven" in Hawaiian, which perfectly describes its staggering scale in the universe.

Key Characteristics

  • Scale: It spans over 520 million light-years in diameter.
  • Mass: It has the mass of approximately 100 quadrillion(100,000,000,000,000,000 / 1017) suns.
  • The Great Attractor: All galaxies within Laniakea are flowing inward toward a mysterious gravitational focal point known as the Great Attractor.

The Structure of Our Cosmic Home

Laniakea is divided into several sub-parts:
  1. Virgo Supercluster: The smaller group that contains our own Milky Way.
  2. Hydra-Centaurus Supercluster: The region where the Great Attractor is located.
  3. Pavo-Indus Supercluster: Another major branch of this cosmic web.

The Cosmic Web

Laniakea is just one "branch" of the larger Cosmic Web. Beyond it lie other massive superclusters like the Shapley Supercluster, toward which Laniakea itself is moving.
  • Filaments: The long strands of galaxies that connect superclusters.
  • Voids: Massive empty spaces between these cosmic threads.
