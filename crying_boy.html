The Crying Boy

Mass-produced in the 1950s, The Crying Boy by Italian artist Giovanni Bragolin is a painting surrounded by a terrifying urban legend. For decades, it has been blamed for causing numerous devastating house fires across the United Kingdom, leaving behind a trail of ashes and modern superstition.

The Curse Unleashed

In 1985, the British newspaper *The Sun* published a shocking article detailing the story of a couple whose home caught fire. The house was completely gutted by flames, yet a printed copy of The Crying Boy remained completely untouched on the blackened wall. This single report triggered global panic.

The Dark Legend of the Child

According to local folklore, the boy in the painting was an orphan named Don Bonillo, whose parents had died in a fire. A priest allegedly warned the artist that wherever the child went, mysterious fires would break out. Shortly after the painting was finished, the artist's studio caught fire, and the boy later died in a tragic accident.

The Unexplained Pattern

  • The Surviving Canvas: Firefighters reported finding multiple copies of the painting completely unburnt amidst the wreckage of destroyed homes.
  • Mass Bonfires: Terrified citizens began dropping off thousands of copies of the artwork at newspaper offices, leading to massive organized bonfires to destroy the "cursed" objects.
  • The Firemen's Jinx: Some firefighters were so shaken by the repeating pattern that they refused to allow any copy of the painting inside their own homes.

A Scientific Explanation

  • Fire-Retardant Material: Investigation showed that the prints were manufactured using thick, high-density hardboard that is naturally difficult to ignite.
  • The Falling Effect: When a fire broke out, the string holding the frame would burn first, causing the painting to fall face down on the floor, protecting the image from oxygen and intense flames.
  • Coincidence and Statistics: As one of the most popular mass-produced paintings in Europe with millions of copies sold, it was statistically likely to be found in at least a few homes experiencing accidents.
