Dwarf Planets

What is a Dwarf Planet?

A dwarf planet is a celestial body that orbits the Sun and has enough mass to assume a nearly round shape. However, unlike a major planet, it has not "cleared its neighborhood" around its orbit, meaning it shares its orbital space with other similar-sized objects like asteroids and comets.

The Reclassification of Pluto

For decades, Pluto was celebrated as the ninth planet from the Sun. However, in 2006, the International Astronomical Union (IAU) redefined what constitutes a "planet." Because Pluto shares its orbital zone with numerous icy bodies in the Kuiper Belt, it was reclassified as a dwarf planet.

Ceres: The Closest Dwarf Planet

Ceres is unique because it is the only dwarf planet located in the inner solar system, nestled inside the Asteroid Belt between Mars and Jupiter. It is also the smallest recognized dwarf planet, yet it makes up a third of the entire mass of the asteroid belt.

Major Dwarf Planets

Pluto The most famous dwarf planet. It has a complex surface featuring icy plains, massive mountains, and a system of five known moons, with Charon being the largest.
Eris Located far beyond Pluto in the scattered disc, Eris is slightly smaller than Pluto but possesses more mass. Its discovery in 2005 triggered the debate that led to Pluto's reclassification.
Haumea & Makemake Haumea is uniquely rugby-ball-shaped due to its incredibly rapid rotation. Makemake, found in the Kuiper Belt, is one of the brightest objects in the outer solar system.
